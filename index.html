Here is the pushback. A 390,000-ton facility would need to capture almost every pound of food waste Miami-Dade throws away. Miami-Dade has no food-waste mandate today, so every ton has to be sold, not forced. Permit and plan the site for 390,000 tons, but build it in modules as contracts land. Yard trash (about 1,011,282 tons a year in the same study) is your carbon source, so the feedstock is there.

The land math decides the rest. By our estimate from BioCycle and vendor design ratios, 390,000 tons needs about 25–60 acres of working pad and 75–150 acres of land once you add curing, storage, stormwater and buffers. At Miami industrial rents that is roughly $10M–$36M a year in rent alone. Inland, the same acreage rents as farmland for a few thousand to about $50,000 a year, or sells for about $1–2.5M.

      The rail plan

      Miami collects. The farm belt composts.

      Load sealed containers at a Miami rail transfer, run them up the FEC to Fort Pierce, and hand them to U.S. Sugar's South Central Florida Express for the run to the Lake Okeechobee farm belt. That's where compost has buyers: sugar cane and vegetable ground on muck soils that lose organic matter every year, plus dairies and citrus. CSX's line through Okeechobee and Sebring is the second route.

      The corridor already moves trash

      • WM ran a pilot shipping waste from its Miami transfer station to its Okeechobee landfill (reported Jan 2024).
      • Hialeah approved a Waste Connections solid waste intermodal rail facility on NW 37th Ct (agenda May 2025).
      • FEC filed for a rail-to-truck trash terminal off Glades Cut-Off Rd in Fort Pierce, feeding the Okeechobee landfill (Army Corps permit pending, Jan 2026).

      What changes for food waste

      • Putrescible waste can sit unprocessed 48 hours at a transfer station, or up to 7 days with an approved vector and odor plan (62-701.710).
      • At the compost site it must be mixed in or removed within 48 hours of receipt (62-709.320).
      • So: sealed intermodal boxes, daily or every-other-day trains, and a receiving pad at the hub sized for a train's worth.

      Partner before you build

      • Your first transfer station should be a contract: tip organics into a rail-served facility someone else already permitted.
      • U.S. Sugar owns the SCFE and the farm belt around it. That's a landlord, a railroad and a compost buyer in one meeting.
      • Ask for a ground lease with an option to buy, and offer compost toward rent.
      The open door

      South Miami's solid waste site, RFP PW2026-04

      This came from the City of South Miami, not the City of Miami. It offers city-owned land at 4795 SW 75th Ave (unincorporated Miami-Dade) on a CSX line for a long-term lease to design, build, finance, operate and maintain a solid waste facility. Proposals closed April 28, 2026. You said no one bid; the city posted a bid tabulation I couldn't open, so confirm the count with procurement.

      What the city wants

      Cost certainty off the county system
      The city is hedging against a less favorable 20-year county disposal deal.
      MSW first
      Strongest preference is a facility that takes all city MSW. Composting, green waste and recovery are welcome, but proposals that don't directly handle city MSW cap at 25 of 35 development points (secondhand summary, verify).
      Rail is on the table
      Proposers had to say whether rail is part of the plan and address STB and Clean Railroads Act issues.

      What it takes to qualify

      • Two years operating under the same entity, two years of similar services, a principal with five years
      • Permitted disposal capacity outside the county system, plus backup plans
      • 5% proposal bond, $25,000 cost-recovery deposit, then at least $50,000 more if selected
      • Performance and payment bonds at 100%, $2M general liability (secondhand summary)

      How you play it

      • You can't meet the operating minimums alone yet. Team with an established hauler or operator as prime, with Melly's running organics.
      • Pitch it as an MSW + organics rail transfer: city MSW to rail, food scraps to the inland hub.
      • Call Steven Kulick, Chief Procurement Officer: Steven.Kulick@SoMi.FL.gov. Ask whether it will be reissued or negotiated directly.
      • Check the parcel against the wellfield map first. A resource recovery facility can't sit in one.
      Rent before you buy

      Lease the land, own the permit

      Leasing keeps cash in the business while you prove volume. The permit, the customer contracts and the operating record are the real assets, and they move with you. Miami land only makes sense for small pieces: a pilot pad and a rail transfer. The big pad belongs where rent is measured in tens of dollars an acre.

      The rulebook

      What each facility has to clear

      The state permits the process, the county permits the operation and guards the groundwater, and the zoning code decides whether the use is allowed on that land at all. Recyclables ride on a lighter, more open set of rules than organics.

      STATE · FDEP 62-709

      Composting organics

      Food scraps need a permit
      Registration covers yard trash, vegetative waste, manure and animal byproducts. Mixed commercial food waste means a solid waste permit. 62-709.320/.350/.500
      48 hours to process
      Putrescibles mixed in or removed within 48 hours. 62-709.320(2)(e)
      Pad, leachate, pathogens
      Asphalt or concrete for receiving, processing and curing; leachate collected; 55 °C for 3 days in-vessel or 15 days in windrows with 5 turnings. 62-709.500(7), .300(8)
      Water setbacks
      No storage within 500 ft of a potable well or 200 ft of water or wetlands. 62-701.300(2)
      Pilot route
      Up to 10,000 yd³ of feedstock to prove a site. 62-709.460
      STATE · FDEP 62-701.710

      Transfer stations and MRFs

      Permit required
      No one builds or runs a transfer station or materials recovery facility without an FDEP permit. (1)(b)
      Putrescible clock
      48 hours unprocessed, or up to 7 days with vector and odor controls in the operation plan. (4)(b)
      Operations
      Leachate control, litter control on open areas, a trained spotter on duty, and a daily log of tons in, processed, stored and out, kept 3 years. (3), (4)(c), (8)
      STATE · F.S. 403.7046

      Paper, cardboard, plastic, aluminum

      Certify every year
      Anyone who handles, buys, receives or sells recovered materials certifies annually with FDEP (fee up to $50) and reports by Feb 1 to FDEP and each county they took material from.
      An open market
      A local government can't force a business to hand its source-separated recyclables to the government. You can compete for commercial paper, OCC, metals and plastics. 403.7046(2)
      Local limits still apply
      Counties can require source separation and certification, and can pass health and safety rules. (2)(a)–(e)
      COUNTY · CH. 24 + CH. 15

      Miami-Dade operations

      No facilities in wellfield zones
      Resource recovery facilities are banned in all 20 Wellfield Protection Areas. Sec. 24-43
      Annual operating permit
      Resource Recovery & Management Facility permit from RER, 305-372-6600, plus a DERM environmental control plan and Solid Waste Dept. approval. Composting haulers need the new hauler permit. 24-47.1, 15-18, 15-17(f)
      Businesses must recycle
      At least 3 of 10 materials: office paper, mixed paper, cardboard, wood, scrap metal, steel, glass, textiles, plastics, aluminum. Fines run $300–$950 a day. Homes and multifamily separate newspaper, glass, aluminum and steel cans, plastics. County brochure; §15-2.6 via third party
      No food-waste mandate found
      As of Nov 2026, nothing forces businesses to divert food scraps. Watch the Zero Waste Master Plan rules.
      ZONING · CH. 33

      Where the use is allowed

      AU land: staff approval for compost
      Since fall 2025, composting on agricultural land skips the public hearing. 33-279(31)
      Under 12,500 tons a year: 25 ft setbacks
      Bigger sites get setbacks set by the Director. Piles max 12 ft. Inside the UDB, the land must be an active farm. 33-279(31)(f),(h),(i)
      Airports
      No compost in airport hazard zones, 5,000 ft from piston airports, aviation approval within 10,000 ft of turbine airports. 33-279(31)(k)–(l)
      Transfer and MRF
      Industrial districts, usually with a public hearing. Medley, Hialeah, Doral and other cities run their own codes.
      RAIL · FEDERAL

      Solid waste by rail

      Railroads don't get a free pass on trash
      Since the Clean Railroads Act of 2008, solid waste rail transfer facilities follow state environmental rules like any other site. The STB can grant a land-use exemption from local zoning in some cases. From general knowledge; confirm with rail counsel.
      Local pushback is real
      Fort Pierce officials said they couldn't block FEC's terminal on zoning. Wetlands still went to the Army Corps.
      Lake O watershed
      Hubs near Lake Okeechobee sit in phosphorus-restricted basins. Expect nutrient questions from FDEP and the water district. Verify basin rules per site.
      Before you sign

      Parcel checklist

      1. Pull the folio: zoning, land use, city or unincorporated.
      2. Overlay the county Wellfield Protection Area map. Any overlap ends it.
      3. Measure 200 ft to canals, lakes and wetlands; 500 ft to potable wells.
      4. Measure to runways: 5,000 ft piston, 10,000 ft turbine.
      5. Inside or outside the UDB. Inside on AU means a working farm.
      6. For rail: is the track active? CSX took the south 12 miles of the Homestead line out of service in 2019.
      7. Confirm the railroad will serve a spur and at what car or container rate.
      8. FEMA flood zone and surge; pads and ponds have to survive a hurricane.
      9. Homes within 1,000 ft. Odor complaints shut compost sites down, not permits.
      10. Pre-application meetings: FDEP district, RER Environmental Permitting, Zoning, DSWM.
      11. Lease terms: option to buy, permit contingency, assignment rights, term long enough to amortize the pad.
